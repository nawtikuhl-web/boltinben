I wasn’t shit at running. I was shit at training.

My original running plan went something like this:

Sign up for an event. Run a little further every time I went out. Hate every second of it. Injure myself. Repeat.

I thought eventually I’d get fit enough for running to stop feeling horrible.

The problem was that I was constantly testing my fitness rather than giving my body a chance to build it.

So I slowed down. Properly slowed down. At first, that meant walking more than my ego was particularly comfortable with.

Six to eight months later, my easy pace had gone from 7:00/km to 5:00/km at the same heart rate.

But the biggest win wasn’t the number on my watch.

It was that running finally felt good. I could go further, recover better and actually look forward to my next run.

I went from someone who ran to someone who loved running.

That’s what I want for you.

A few things worth knowing.

Do I need a running watch?

No. Although I recommend a heart rate monitor, particularly if you’re new to easy running. The book explains how to train and measure your progress using perceived effort if you don’t have one.

Am I too unfit for this?

Nope. The first plan is designed around run-walk intervals for people who can barely run continuously.

Walking is training. Get comfortable with that.

How many days a week do I need to run?

Three or four, depending on your starting point, with an optional fifth at the highest level. Your long run is on Saturday and Sunday is always off.

How much faster will I get?

I’d be talking out of my arse if I gave you a specific number.

Everyone starts somewhere different and everyone adapts at a different rate. That’s exactly why we measure your individual progress instead of chasing an arbitrary pace.

What if I miss a run?

Skip it. Miss a whole week? Repeat the last one you completed. There’s no catching up or punishment running.

What happens after eight weeks?

Take an easy week and move to the next starting point when you’re ready. All three plans are included. Once you’ve built your base, you can start introducing more speed or training towards a race.
